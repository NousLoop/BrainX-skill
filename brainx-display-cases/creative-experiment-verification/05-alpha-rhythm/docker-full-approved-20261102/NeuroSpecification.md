## Researcher request
- Brain-modeling question or behavior: create a resting cortical circuit with an alpha-like rhythm and determine how its simulated EEG changes as inhibition is weakened.
- Requested model, experiment, or comparison: a single Jansen-Rit cortical-column neural mass with interacting pyramidal, excitatory-interneuron, and inhibitory-interneuron populations. Scale only inhibitory postsynaptic gain `Ai` to 100%, 75%, 50%, and 25% of its 22 mV baseline. Compare all four conditions across the same eight deterministic initial conditions.
- Execution mode: forward-simulation.
- Required outputs: unit-aware BrainX code; raw population states and EEG proxies; per-condition spectra and paired metrics; numerical and observation-mapping controls; independently reviewed results; final scientific figure and report.
- Constraints: use `brainmass.JansenRitStep` to represent the interacting cortical populations and its public `eeg()` cortical EEG proxy, `brainstate.transform.for_loop` for time evolution, `brainstate.transform.vmap` for independent inhibition/initial-condition lanes, and `brainunit` quantities for time, postsynaptic gains, potentials, and firing-rate drive. Use a 0.1 ms production step, 6 s duration, discard the first 2 s, and analyze the remaining 4 s. Use float64 when available. Fix all non-intervention parameters before inspecting production outcomes.
- Open decisions and handling: none. The agent chose the model family, parameters, initial-condition ensemble, solver, duration, observables, metrics, thresholds, and controls under the explicit delegation. Step-1 study corrected the initially named Wilson-Cowan family to Jansen-Rit before implementation because BrainMass documents Jansen-Rit as its alpha/EEG cortical-column model and exposes unit-bearing inhibitory gain and EEG State; the correction is recorded in the append-only workflow memory. All choices are fixed in the immutable production configuration before the production run.

## Inspected data contract
- Data sources and inspected contents: no empirical dataset or target recording was supplied. Inputs are constant resting external drives and the locked parameter table in the run configuration.
- Shapes, axes, sampling/time base, and physical units: raw arrays use `[time, condition, initial_condition]` for each observable; four inhibition conditions and eight paired initial conditions; 0.1 ms sampling; time in ms/s, external pyramidal input rate in Hz, inverse synaptic time constants in Hz (equivalently time constants in ms), postsynaptic gains and population potentials in mV, dimensionless intracolumn connection counts, and cortical EEG proxy in mV.
- Required preprocessing and the subset used to fit each transform: no fitting or learned transform. Remove only the locked 2 s transient and subtract each retained trace's time mean before spectral or RMS analysis. Use the same Hann-window Welch settings for every lane.
- Mapping from data to model inputs, targets, and observables: a constant 220 Hz external input drives the pyramidal population. The primary observable is the demeaned public `JansenRitStep.eeg()`/`M` cortical EEG proxy in mV, representing the difference between excitatory and inhibitory postsynaptic potentials at pyramidal cells. Save the excitatory and inhibitory potential components when the public model exposes them so their difference can be checked against the proxy. This is a cortical source proxy, not predicted scalp microvolts.
- Known data limitations or unresolved mismatches: no empirical calibration, volume conductor, thalamus, laminar geometry, conduction delay, or sensor noise is represented. Parameter values define a phenomenological alpha regime rather than an individual or species estimate.
- Conditional interpretations of unanswered experimental questions: “weaken inhibition” is represented only as reduced effective I-to-E coupling. Results do not distinguish receptor efficacy, interneuron recruitment, synapse number, or other biological routes that produce the same effective change.

## Acceptance boundary
- Evidence required for success, failure, or an inconclusive result: after 10× analysis decimation to 1 ms, a lane has an identifiable oscillation only when retained EEG RMS is at least 0.01 mV. Baseline succeeds as alpha-like when at least 7/8 retained primary-proxy traces are finite, exceed that floor, and have their dominant 4–30 Hz Welch peak in 8–13 Hz, while the baseline median alpha-band (8–13 Hz) power fraction within 4–30 Hz is at least 0.50 and median RMS is at least 0.05 mV. Welch uses 2 s Hann segments with 50% overlap, giving 0.5 Hz bins. The intervention is considered to produce a clear change when at least one weakened condition differs from paired baseline by at least 10% in median primary-proxy RMS or by at least 0.5 Hz in median peak frequency, with the paired direction shared by at least 6/8 initial conditions. Report the actual direction and any non-monotonicity; do not require a preconceived direction.
- Required baselines and controls: matched 100% inhibition baseline; identical 220 Hz drive, untouched model defaults, sampling, and initial-condition pairs across conditions; eight-condition nuisance ensemble formed by setting initial `M` to `[-2.0, -1.5, -1.0, -0.5, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0] mV` while setting every other dynamical State to unit-preserving zero; component check that public `eeg()` agrees with the documented excitatory-minus-inhibitory potential mapping when those component States are available; half-step (0.05 ms) convergence reruns for 100% and 25% inhibition using the first initial condition, requiring peak-frequency difference no larger than 0.5 Hz and retained RMS relative difference no larger than 5%; explicit finite-value and plausible-bound check on retained EEG magnitude (absolute value below 100 mV). Save per-lane results rather than only aggregates.
- Invalid-result conditions: any unhandled NaN/Inf; unit or shape failure; intervention changes more than declared `Ai`; baseline fails the locked alpha criterion; retained EEG exceeds the locked plausible bound; production configuration is changed after inspecting intervention outcomes; or half-step convergence fails. A stable fixed point in a weakened condition is a valid result, not an invalid run.
- Allowed claims and explicit non-claims: allowed—within this fixed phenomenological model, scaling effective inhibitory feedback is sufficient (or insufficient) to change the cortical EEG proxy in the measured way, and the paired initial-condition ensemble describes sensitivity to starting state. Not allowed—unique identification of the biological mechanism of reduced inhibition, clinical seizure prediction, universal cortical behavior, empirical parameter validity, literal scalp-voltage amplitude, or proof that inhibition loss causes the same change in vivo.
- Model/comparison rationale and decisive assumption checks: the smallest package-native model jointly preserving cortical population interactions, a known alpha regime, a unit-bearing inhibitory-strength intervention, and an interpretable EEG proxy is `JansenRitStep`: `reduced inhibitory postsynaptic gain -> changed cortical-column population dynamics -> changed pyramidal EEG proxy`. Only `Ai` varies, so intervention and control are structurally distinguishable. The component mapping is checked directly and every intervention is paired over the same initial `M` ensemble. A future empirical distinction among biological causes would require separate measurement of interneuron firing and inhibitory postsynaptic efficacy; this simulation cannot identify them.
